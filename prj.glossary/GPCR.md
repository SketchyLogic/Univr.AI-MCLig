# GPCR

**G protein-coupled receptor** — a large superfamily of cell-surface receptor proteins defined by a characteristic architecture of seven α-helices that span the cell membrane (hence also called 7-transmembrane or 7TM receptors). When an extracellular ligand (hormone, neurotransmitter, odorant, photon) binds, the receptor changes shape and activates an intracellular G protein, which relays the signal further into the cell.

GPCRs are the largest family of membrane receptors in the human genome (~800 members) and are involved in regulating virtually every physiological process. Approximately 20% of all FDA-approved drugs work by binding to a GPCR. Their structural study is challenging because the hydrophobic transmembrane domains resist standard purification and crystallisation methods, creating a supply-demand gap between known sequences and solved 3D structures.

**Analogy:** Think of a GPCR as a doorbell button embedded in a wall (the membrane). When pressed (ligand binds outside), a signal travels through the wall and rings a bell inside the cell (G-protein activation).

## TLDR
A family of 7-helix membrane receptors; targets of ~20% of drugs; hard to crystallise; often modelled computationally.
