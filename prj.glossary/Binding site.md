# Binding site

A specific region on a protein's surface or interior where a ligand (small molecule, peptide, another protein) binds. The binding site is typically a cavity or groove shaped to complement the ligand's geometry and chemistry. The key residues lining the site make non-covalent interactions with the ligand: hydrogen bonds, electrostatic interactions, van der Waals contacts, and hydrophobic packing.

**Types:**
- **Orthosteric site:** The primary, evolutionarily conserved binding site where the natural (endogenous) ligand binds.
- **Allosteric site:** A secondary site distinct from the orthosteric site; ligands binding here alter the protein's shape and activity indirectly.
- **Cryptic site:** A site that only becomes accessible when the protein moves — not visible in static crystal structures.

**In drug discovery:** Identifying and characterising binding sites is the first step in structure-based drug design. Whether a site is "druggable" (able to bind a small molecule with sufficient affinity and selectivity) is a critical question.

## TLDR
The region of a protein where a ligand docks; can be orthosteric, allosteric, or cryptic; the primary target in drug design.
