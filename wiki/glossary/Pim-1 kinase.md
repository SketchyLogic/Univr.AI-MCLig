# Pim-1 kinase

**Pim-1** is a serine/threonine protein kinase encoded by the *PIM1* proto-oncogene (Proviral Integration site for Moloney murine leukemia virus). It promotes cell survival, proliferation, and resistance to apoptosis. Overexpression is found in haematological malignancies (acute myeloid leukaemia, diffuse large B-cell lymphoma) and solid tumours (prostate cancer), making it a validated oncology drug target.

## Structure and binding pocket

Pim-1 adopts the canonical bilobal kinase fold — a smaller N-terminal lobe and a larger C-terminal lobe connected by a hinge region — and competes with ATP for the same binding cleft. A structurally unusual feature is a **proline residue** (Pro123 in the human sequence) in the hinge. This proline lacks the backbone NH that most kinases use to hydrogen-bond with the adenine ring of ATP or inhibitors. As a result, classical ATP-competitive inhibitors cannot anchor via the adenine–hinge interaction that works in most kinase drug programs; ligand designs must adopt alternative geometries that still achieve affinity.

This quirk means the Pim-1 pocket demands careful attention to protein flexibility: small changes in ligand scaffold can shift the pocket geometry significantly, a challenge that AI-MCLig is designed to handle by re-predicting the protein conformation at every [[Monte Carlo simulation]] step.
